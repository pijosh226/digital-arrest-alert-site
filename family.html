Your parent is on a long call with a number they don't know

You're here because Digital Arrest Alert on their phone gave them a message to send you. It takes one minute to read this. Then ring them.

Ring them now. Keep ringing until they answer. Your call is what breaks the scam.

When they pick up, say this

  1. "Hang up on the other call now. I'm coming." Say it before anything else. Don't ask what's happening yet.
  2. "You are not in trouble. No police arrests anyone on a video call." Say it twice if needed. The scammer has spent an hour convincing them otherwise.
  3. "Don't send any money. Don't share any OTP." If they already did, don't scold — ask which bank and how much, then go to the next section.
  4. Stay on the line until you're sure the other call is finished, or until someone is physically with them.

If money has already moved

  1. Ring 1930 (India's cyber-fraud helpline) from their phone or yours, say "cyber fraud, money transferred", note the reference number. The first hour is when a transfer can still be frozen.
  2. Ring the bank's fraud line (the app's After tab has the verified numbers) and ask for a hold and a recall.
  3. File at cybercrime.gov.in with the 1930 reference. Report the caller's number on Sanchar Saathi (Chakshu).
  4. Anyone who now rings offering to recover the money for a fee is the second scam. Always.

Afterwards

Being targeted is not their fault; these are professional criminals. If they feel ashamed or shaken, India's free senior-citizen helpline Elder Line 14567 offers emotional support. Keep the app installed: the guard resets and keeps watching.
