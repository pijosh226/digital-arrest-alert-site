Privacy Policy

Digital Arrest Alert (Android) · Effective 28 September 2026

Digital Arrest Alert is an app for people who might receive a "digital arrest" scam call. When a number that is not in your contacts keeps you on a long call, it shows you a warning card, can ring the family member you chose, and gets a message ready for your family that you send with one tap. This policy explains exactly what the app touches and what leaves your phone.

The short version: no account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking. Everything the app keeps stays on your phone. Things leave your phone only when they go to the family you chose — a phone call, a message you send, or a screen picture you choose to send.

Independent app. Digital Arrest Alert is not made by, endorsed by or linked to any government, police force, bank or the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C). The helpline numbers and websites it shows (for example 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in in India) are official public services of your country.

The app does not have permission to send SMS by itself. When your family should be told, the message opens ready to send in your messaging app or WhatsApp, and you tap Send.

Information stored on your phone

Your name, your mobile number, up to three family numbers with names and how each is told (text, WhatsApp or both), numbers you mark as trusted, your bank's phone number (optional), your country and language, the alert settings, a list of the calls the guard watched in the last 14 days (time, length, calling app, the name or number shown, and whether it was saved; at most 80 calls), anything you type into the evidence notes, and whether you have bought the app. All of it is kept in the app's private storage, excluded from Android backups, and deleted when you uninstall the app. You can clear the evidence notes in the app at any time.

Information that leaves your phone

  • Calls to your family, placed by your phone to the number you chose.
  • Messages to your family, only when you tap Send in your messaging app or WhatsApp. The message says who you are (the name you typed), that you have been on a long call with an unknown number or pressed the help button, and your number so they can ring you back.
  • A screen picture, only if you choose to take and send one.
  • Evidence notes and the call list, only if you tap Share on the evidence screen and choose where to send them (for example to the police or your bank).
  • Read aloud: the app uses your phone's speech engine. On the Learn pages only, and only while the phone is online, it may ask Google's speech engine for a natural online voice; the text sent is the app's own public guidance, never your data. Google's privacy policy applies to its speech engine.

The app has no account, no server of its own, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The developer receives none of your information.

Payment

After a 7-day free trial the app offers a one-time purchase through Google Play. Google processes the payment and holds your payment details; the app only receives Google Play's answer about whether the purchase exists and stores it on your phone. The developer never sees your card, UPI or bank details. Codes for the 3-phone licence are checked on the phone, without any server.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Your choices

Every permission can be refused or withdrawn in Android settings; the app keeps working with reduced protection and says so on its home screen. You can edit or delete family numbers, trusted numbers and evidence notes at any time. Uninstalling removes all data.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published at this address with a new effective date. A feature that would send information over the internet is never switched on without this policy being updated first.
